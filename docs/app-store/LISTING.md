## Description

Lookplay puts the game console you already own on a screen in your room, as large as you like.

Show the app where your console is once. From then on a Connect button floats above it whenever you look at it: pinch, and its picture opens in front of you. Drag the corner to resize it, or pick Small, Standard, Large or Cinema.

• Look to connect. No menus: look at your console and pinch.
• A screen any size. From a desk monitor to a cinema wall, always 16:9.
• Your controller, all of it. Every button, both sticks, both triggers and rumble, read directly from the controller paired with your headset.
• Sharp and quick. Up to 1080p at 60 pictures a second over your home Wi-Fi.
• Private. No account, no tracking, nothing leaves your home network.
• Try it first. A built-in demo shows the screen and your controller with nothing else set up.

WHAT YOU NEED
• A PlayStation®5 console with Remote Play turned on.
• A Mac on the same network running the free, open-source Lookplay bridge, which passes the console's picture to your headset. Set-up takes a few minutes; the guide is linked in the app.
• A wireless controller paired with your Vision Pro.

Lookplay streams only from a console you own, on your own network. It does not include or sell games.

## Export compliance

The app uses only the encryption built into visionOS (HTTPS), which is exempt.
`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` is already set to `false`, so App Store Connect
does not ask.

## Screenshots

Vision Pro screenshots must be 3840 × 2160, and one is enough. Take them from the
app's own demo, not from a game: a game's artwork belongs to its publisher, and
Apple turns listings away for it. In the simulator or on the headset, open
**Try the demo** and capture the view (on the headset: press the top button and
the Digital Crown together). Good ones to have: the first window, the demo on a
Large screen, and the Connect button over the console's spot.

## Notes for App Review

Paste this into **App Review Information → Notes**:

> Lookplay is a remote desktop client for a game console the user owns
> (guideline 4.2.7). It shows the console's picture in a resizable window and
> sends the user's controller input back. The console and the host Mac are on
> the user's own local network; nothing is streamed from the internet, and the
> app contains no store, catalogue or account.
>
> TESTING WITHOUT A CONSOLE: on the first window choose "Try the demo". It
> opens the same screen window the console uses, with a small built-in scene.
> The size buttons under the window (Small, Standard, Large, Cinema) and the
> corner resize work as they do with a console. With a game controller paired to
> the headset, the left stick steers, R2 speeds up, and any face button makes
> the controller rumble. With no controller the scene flies by itself.
>
> "Show the app where your PS5 is" opens a mixed immersive space with one blue
> ring: drag it onto any object and choose Done. A Connect button then appears
> above that spot whenever you look towards it.
>
> WITH A CONSOLE: the user runs our open-source bridge on a Mac on the same
> network (https://github.com/THEMANINHOLLYWOOD/ps5-vision). The app finds it
> with Bonjour (_ps5vision._tcp), which is why it asks for Local Network
> access. World Sensing is used only to keep a world anchor at the console's
> position. No login is needed to review the app.
>
> The app is not affiliated with Sony. "PS5" is used only to say which console
> it works with.
